Core Mathematical Formulations Decoded: Theoretical Rationale and Mechanics

A structured mathematical primer explaining the role of each governing equation in the Riemannian Stiefel IRLS solver and precision allocation framework:

Equation 1: Outlier Suppression via Cauchy Redescending M-Estimator

Manuscript Eq. (13)
$$w_{ij} = \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{r_{ij}}{c \cdot \sigma}\right)^2}, \qquad \sigma = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{median}\Big(|r_{ij} - \operatorname{median}(r)|\Big)$$

Statistical Rationale: In standard least-squares ($L_2$ loss), the derivative is linear, giving unbounded leverage to gross outliers. The Cauchy loss $\rho(z) = \frac{c^2}{2} \ln(1 + z^2/c^2)$ has redescending derivative $\psi(z) = z / (1 + z^2/c^2)$. When an observation residual exceeds the dispersion threshold ($c \sigma$), the weight $w_{ij}$ smoothly drops towards zero at rate $\mathcal{O}(1/r^2)$.

Operational Impact: Outliers and gross sensor spikes receive near-zero weights (median $w \approx 0.0024$), effectively expelling corrupted entries from the loss and preventing the high-precision FP32 budget from being squandered on faulty data.

Equation 2: Entrywise Riemannian Gradient Perturbation Norm

Manuscript Eq. (3)
$$\|\delta G_{ij}\|_F = 2 \, W_{ij} \, |E_{ij}| \, \|v_j\|_2$$

Structural Derivation: This bound decouples into three distinct physical and geometric factors:

  • $W_{ij}$ (Robust Statistics): Inlier confidence. Outlier entries have $W_{ij} \approx 0$ and exert zero gradient influence, needing no high-precision protection.
  • $|E_{ij}|$ (Hardware Quantization): Rounding discrepancy introduced by low-precision representation ($E_{ij} = Y_{ij} - Q_s(Y_{ij})$).
  • $\|v_j\|_2$ (Subspace Geometry): Statistical leverage and norm of column factor $v_j$.

Synthesis: The product upper-bounds the Riemannian gradient perturbation $\lVert\delta G_{ij}\rVert_F$. Protecting the top-$k$ scores directly minimizes worst-case solver disturbance.

Equation 3: Residual-Influence Allocation Score

Allocation Policy
$$s_{ij} = W_{ij} \cdot |R^0_{ij}| \cdot \|v_j\|_2, \qquad H = \operatorname{arg\,top-}k (s_{ij})$$

Mechanism: Evaluates entrywise gradient leverage based on current residuals $|r_{ij}|$. In dynamic reallocation under ultra-low-bit formats (FP4), this stabilizes factor trajectories and focuses protection on active learning boundaries.

Equation 4: Closed-Form Weighted Least Squares for Factor V

IRLS V-Update
$$\left(U_{\Omega_j}^\top W_j U_{\Omega_j} + \lambda I_r\right) v_j = U_{\Omega_j}^\top \left(W_j \widehat{y}_{\Omega_j}\right)$$

Computational Efficiency: Because $U \in \mathrm{St}(m, r)$ has orthonormal columns ($U^\top U = I_r$), the V-subproblem decouples into $n$ independent, well-conditioned $r \times r$ symmetric linear systems that solve directly in closed form.

Equation 5: Riemannian Tangent Projection & Polar Retraction

Differential Geometry
$$P_{T_U}(G) = G - U \operatorname{sym}(U^\top G), \qquad R_U(\xi) = \operatorname{polar}(U + \xi) = L R^\top$$

Geometric Rationale: Constraining $U$ to $\mathrm{St}(m, r)$ eliminates the scaling gauge ambiguity $UV^\top = (US)(VS^{-\top})^\top$, preventing factor magnitude drift in low-bit storage. The tangent projection $P_{T_U}(G)$ projects the Euclidean gradient onto the Stiefel tangent space, while the polar retraction $R_U(\xi) = \mathrm{polar}(U + \xi) = LR^\top$ pulls trial iterates back onto the manifold.

Conceptual Overview: Low-Rank Recovery & 4-Bit Storage

A step-by-step intuitive guide illustrating how mixed-precision routing solves the memory-bandwidth bottleneck in robust scientific matrix recovery.

🖼️
1. The Spatiotemporal Sensing Grid
Continuous physical sensor networks (such as highway traffic flow or air quality grids) form structured low-rank spatiotemporal matrices. Environmental outages cause unobserved voids (missing data), and hardware glitches inject corruptions (outliers).
📦
2. The Hardware Memory Wall
Storing millions of continuous sensor measurements in full-precision FP32 or FP64 overwhelms memory bandwidth. Compressing all entries into ultra-coarse 4-bit formats (FP4) provides an 8x memory footprint reduction, but uniform 4-bit storage leads to severe numerical error explosions.
🕵️‍♂️
3. Influence-Driven Precision Routing
Instead of uniform precision, we establish a dual-pool memory hierarchy: the vast majority of entries are compressed into 4-bit storage, while a tiny protected fraction (e.g. 10%–30%) of high-leverage, high-error entries are preserved in full FP32 precision.
🚫
4. Robust Outlier Rejection via Cauchy IRLS
When severe sensor spikes occur (e.g. telemetry errors reading 9999), standard least-squares collapses. The redescending Cauchy loss automatically assigns near-zero weights ($w \to 0$) to gross outliers, suppressing their influence without manual filtering.
⚖️
5. Column-Balanced Fairness Quotas
Under aggressive 4-bit storage, stations with fewer observations risk numerical rank starvation. Column-balanced quotas guarantee every sensor station a minimum high-precision share, preserving the condition number of column subproblems.
🎉
6. Near-Lossless 4-Bit Storage
With only 10%–30% FP32 protection, the solver matches full double-precision accuracy to reported precision on physical traffic and climate grids, unlocking over 56%–75% active memory savings.
